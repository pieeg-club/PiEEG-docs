# Connection & Pinout

## What's on the board

![EasyEXG parts: J1, J2, J3, U1, U2, JP1](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_annotated.jpg)

| Label | Part | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **J1** | 3.5 mm audio jack (CUI SJ-3523-SMT) | Plug for a standard 3-lead snap-electrode cable: Tip = IN+, Ring = IN−, Sleeve = REF/DRL |
| **J2** | 3-pin header, 2.54 mm (optional) | Same three electrode signals as J1, for loose wires: + / − / REF |
| **U1** | TI INA333 | Instrumentation amplifier, first gain stage (9.9×) |
| **U2** | TI OPA4388 | Quad op-amp: VCC/2 reference, driven right leg, 0.16 Hz high-pass + gain, 500 Hz low-pass |
| **JP1** | Solder jumper "HI GAIN" | Open = 221× (EMG, EOG). Bridged = 1 048× (ECG, EEG) |
| **J3** | 4-pin header, 2.54 mm | GND · VCC · OUT · VREF |
| **H1, H2** | 2 × M2 mounting holes, Ø 2.2 mm | 40.2 mm apart |

![EasyEXG connection overview](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_connection.png)

## Electrode inputs

![IN+, IN− and REF on the jack J1 and the header J2](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_inputs.png)

Both connectors carry the same three signals. **Use one at a time.**

| Signal | J1 · 3.5 mm jack | J2 · 3 pins (silkscreen) | Purpose |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **IN+** | Tip | pin 1 · "+" (square pad) | Positive measuring electrode |
| **IN−** | Ring | pin 2 · "−" | Negative measuring electrode |
| **REF** | Sleeve | pin 3 · "REF" | Reference / driven right leg: cancels mains hum |

<Callout type="info">
  Wire colours differ between electrode cables. Follow the **plug position** (Tip / Ring / Sleeve), not the colour. If you're unsure, check with a multimeter in continuity mode: tip of the plug → which snap.
</Callout>

The amplifier measures **IN+ minus IN−**. Swapping them only flips the signal upside down. The **REF** electrode is actively driven by the board (driven right leg, current limited to about 13 µA by a 390 kΩ resistor) and keeps the body at the board's mid-supply level, which removes most 50/60 Hz hum.

## Output header J3

| Pin | Name | Connect to | Notes |
|:---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **GND** | MCU GND | Square pad |
| 2 | **VCC** | MCU 5 V or 3.3 V | Use the same voltage as the MCU's ADC range. Max 5.5 V |
| 3 | **OUT** | MCU analog input (A0) | Rests at VCC/2, swings with the biosignal |
| 4 | **VREF** | Not needed | VCC/2 reference, optional: use it for a differential ADC or to subtract the offset in hardware |

![J3 pinout on the bottom silkscreen](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_pinout_bottom.jpg)

The pinout and the jack wiring are also printed on the bottom of the board.

## Wiring to your microcontroller

Always power EasyEXG from the **same voltage as your MCU's ADC range**. The output swings between 0 V and VCC, so a 5 V supply on a 3.3 V board can push more than 3.3 V into its analog pin.

| MCU | VCC from | OUT to | GND | Software |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arduino UNO R4 WiFi** | 5V | A0 | GND | USB or Wi-Fi sketch |
| **Arduino UNO / Nano / Mega** | 5V | A0 | GND | USB sketch |
| **ESP32** | 3V3 | GPIO36 (VP, A0) | GND | Wi-Fi sketch |
| **ESP8266** | 3V3 | A0 | GND | Wi-Fi sketch |
| **Raspberry Pi Pico** (Arduino-Pico core) | 3V3(OUT), pin 36 | GP26 / ADC0, pin 31 | GND, pin 38 | USB sketch |
| **STM32** (Arduino core) | 3V3 | Any ADC pin, e.g. PA0 / A0 | GND | USB sketch (change `PIN`) |

<Callout type="error">
  On an ESP32 or ESP8266, connect VCC to **3.3 V**. Their analog inputs take 3.3 V max. In the Python viewer set `VREF = 3.3` so the µV values are correct.
</Callout>

![Wiring to a Raspberry Pi Pico](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_wiring_pico.png)

Install the **Raspberry Pi Pico/RP2040** board package (Arduino-Pico core) in the Boards Manager, then upload the USB sketch unchanged: `A0` is GP26 on the Pico.

## Gain jumper JP1

![JP1 open and bridged](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_jp1_gain.png)

| JP1 | Total gain | Use for | Max input before clipping (5 V / 3.3 V) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** (as shipped) | **221×** | EMG, EOG | 10.9 mV / 7.2 mV |
| **Bridged** | **1 048×** | ECG, EEG | 2.3 mV / 1.5 mV |

**To bridge:** with the board unpowered, heat both pads with a clean soldering iron and add a small blob of solder so it joins them.
**To open again:** remove the solder with solder wick.

![JP1 and the signal chain on the board](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_configuration.png)

<Callout type="info">
  The gain in your software must match JP1, otherwise the µV values are 4.7× too big or too small. The Python viewer has a **Gain** setting (221 / 1048).
</Callout>
