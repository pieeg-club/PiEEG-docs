# EasyEXG

**Muscle, heart, eye and brain signals on any ADC pin.**

EasyEXG is a small open-source analog front end for biosignals. Plug in a standard 3-lead electrode cable, connect three wires to your microcontroller, and read **EMG** (muscle), **ECG** (heart), **EOG** (eye movement) or **EEG** (brain rhythm) with a plain `analogRead()`. It runs from **3.3 V or 5 V** and gives one analog output centred at VCC/2, so it works with Arduino, ESP32, ESP8266, STM32, Raspberry Pi Pico and any other board with an ADC.

![EasyEXG: electrodes on the body, EasyEXG, signals on any microcontroller](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_how_it_works.jpg)

<Callout type="warning">
  EasyEXG is **not a medical device**. It has **no isolation**: while electrodes are on a person, everything connected to it must run **on battery** (power bank, laptop with the charger unplugged). Read [Safety](/hardware/devices/easy_exg/safety) first.
</Callout>

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Channels** | 1 differential input (IN+, IN−) plus reference / driven right leg |
| **Signals** | EMG, ECG, EOG, EEG |
| **Amplifiers** | TI INA333 instrumentation amplifier + TI OPA4388 quad op-amp |
| **Gain** | 221× (JP1 open) or 1 048× (JP1 bridged) |
| **Bandwidth** | 0.16–500 Hz (−3 dB) |
| **Output** | 1 analog signal, centred at VCC/2 |
| **Supply** | 3.3 V or 5 V (max 5.5 V) |
| **Electrodes** | 3.5 mm jack for 3-lead snap cables, or a 3-pin 2.54 mm header |
| **Host** | Any microcontroller with an ADC (not included) |
| **Size** | 46 × 26 mm, 2 × M2 mounting holes |
| **Software** | Arduino sketches (USB and Wi-Fi) + Python viewer |

## How it works

```
 IN+ ─┐
 IN− ─┼─► EasyEXG ──OUT (analog)──► ADC of your MCU ──USB or Wi-Fi──► Serial Plotter
 REF ─┘   INA333 + OPA4388           Arduino · ESP32 ·                  or Python viewer
          gain 221× / 1 048×         STM32 · Pico
          0.16–500 Hz
```

1. **Amplify:** the INA333 picks up the tiny difference between IN+ and IN− (microvolts to millivolts) and rejects mains hum (CMRR ≥ 100 dB).
2. **Filter:** the OPA4388 removes slow drift (0.16 Hz high-pass), adds more gain and cuts noise above 500 Hz. It also drives the reference electrode to cancel common-mode noise (driven right leg).
3. **Read:** the output sits at VCC/2 with no signal and swings up and down with the biosignal. Your microcontroller samples it with its ADC.

## What you can measure

| Signal | What it shows | JP1 | Typical first test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EMG** | Muscle activity | Open · 221× | Clench your fist: bursts appear |
| **ECG** | Heart activity | Bridged · 1 048× | Electrodes on the chest: a heartbeat every second |
| **EOG** | Eye movement | Open · 221× | Look left and right: the trace steps |
| **EEG** | Brain rhythm (alpha) | Bridged · 1 048× | Close your eyes: alpha (8–12 Hz) grows |

## EasyEXG or ardEEG?

| | EasyEXG | ardEEG |
|---|---|---|
| **Channels** | 1 | 8 |
| **Output** | Analog, read by your MCU's ADC | Digital, 24-bit ADS1299 |
| **Host** | Any MCU with an ADC | Arduino UNO R4 WiFi |
| **Best for** | EMG, ECG, EOG, first EEG experiments, embedded projects | Multi-channel EEG |
| **Electrodes** | 3-lead snap cable (3.5 mm jack) | 8 electrodes + 2 ear clips |
