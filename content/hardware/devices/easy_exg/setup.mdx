# How to Start

From the box to a live muscle signal (EMG) in the Arduino Serial Plotter, in six steps. The same steps work for ECG, EOG and EEG: only the jumper and the electrode positions change.

![The four steps: wiring, electrodes, upload, read the signal](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_arduino.gif)

<Steps>

### Gather the parts

You need the EasyEXG, an Arduino (any UNO, Nano or UNO R4), 3 jumper wires, a 3-lead snap cable with a 3.5 mm plug, 3 gel electrodes and a USB cable. See [What's in the Box](/hardware/devices/easy_exg/required-equipment).

### Set the gain jumper JP1

| Measurement | JP1 | Gain |
|---|---|---|
| **EMG** (muscle), **EOG** (eyes) | **Open** (as shipped) | 221× |
| **ECG** (heart), **EEG** (brain) | **Bridged** with a solder blob | 1 048× |

For this first test (EMG), leave JP1 **open**. Details: [Gain jumper JP1](/hardware/devices/easy_exg/connections#gain-jumper-jp1).

### Wire J3 to the Arduino

Use three jumper wires, with the Arduino unplugged:

| EasyEXG pin (J3) | Arduino pin | Wire colour in the pictures |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **GND** | GND | grey |
| **VCC** | 5V (or 3.3V) | red |
| **OUT** | A0 | yellow |
| **VREF** | not connected | — |

![EasyEXG wired to an Arduino UNO R4 WiFi](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_wiring_arduino.png)

Using an ESP32 or a Raspberry Pi Pico? Power EasyEXG from **3.3 V**, see [Connection & Pinout](/hardware/devices/easy_exg/connections#wiring-to-your-microcontroller).

### Upload the sketch

1. Install the [Arduino IDE](https://www.arduino.cc/en/software).
2. Open `EasyEXG_Arduino.ino` from the [`Arduino`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/EasyEXG/tree/main/Arduino) folder of the EasyEXG repository.
3. Plug the Arduino into the computer, choose your board and port in **Tools**, and click **Upload**.

The sketch samples A0 at **500 Hz**, removes the resting level (about 512 counts = VCC/2) and prints two numbers per line: the **signal** and its **envelope** (muscle activity level).

### Place the electrodes

Clean the skin with an alcohol wipe, snap the electrodes onto the cable and stick them on:

| Electrode | Plug contact | Where (EMG, forearm) |
|---|---|---|
| **IN+** | Tip | On the forearm muscle, a hand-width from the wrist |
| **IN−** | Ring | Same muscle, 2–3 cm further along |
| **REF** | Sleeve | On a bony spot: the elbow |

Plug the cable into the **3.5 mm jack J1**.

![EMG electrode placement on the forearm](/images/hardware/2026/11/easyexg/easyexg_app_emg.gif)

<Callout type="error">
  EasyEXG has **no isolation**. Before the electrodes go on, **unplug the laptop charger** so the laptop runs on battery, or switch to the Wi-Fi sketch and power the Arduino from a power bank. Never record from a computer plugged into the mains.
</Callout>

### Read the signal

Open **Tools → Serial Plotter** and set **115200 baud**.

| Test | What you should see |
|---|---|
| **Relax the arm** | Both traces close to flat |
| **Clench your fist** | Bursts on the blue trace (signal), the yellow trace (envelope) rises |
| **Relax again** | Both traces go flat |

</Steps>
