# Signals & Datasets

What real PiEEG-16 recordings look like, open data to practise with, and a first machine-learning project. All recordings below were made with **dry electrodes, no gel**, at 250 samples per second.

## Example signals

### EEG: alpha test

Eyes closed for 5 seconds, open for 5 seconds, repeated. The signal is band-pass filtered to 8–12 Hz, so the alpha rhythm shows up as bursts of larger amplitude while the eyes are closed. Dataset: [`3.Alpha_test.xlsx`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/PiEEG-16/blob/main/Dataset/3.Alpha_test.xlsx).

![16 channels of alpha-band EEG from PiEEG-16](/images/hardware/2026/10/pieeg16_alpha.png)

The same test with a wavelet transform. The bright bands in the lower plot are the eyes-closed periods.

![Alpha test with wavelet transform](/images/hardware/2026/10/pieeg16_alpha_wavelet.png)

### EEG: chewing and blinking artifacts

Chewing 4, 3, 2 and 1 times, then blinking 4, 3, 2 and 1 times. The signal is band-pass filtered to 1–40 Hz. These large spikes come from muscles and eye movement, not the brain. Dataset: [`2.Chewing_Blinking.xlsx`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/PiEEG-16/blob/main/Dataset/2.Chewing_Blinking.xlsx).

![Chewing and blinking artifacts on 16 channels](/images/hardware/2026/10/pieeg16_artifacts.png)

<Callout type="info">
  Learn to recognise artifacts before you analyse anything. Remove or mark them before computing band power or training a model.
</Callout>

## Datasets

All datasets are in the [`Dataset`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/PiEEG-16/tree/main/Dataset) folder of the PiEEG-16 repository. Raw data in µV, 16 channels, 250 SPS, dry Ag/AgCl electrodes without gel.

| File | Content | Length |
|---|---|---|
| `1.Chewing_Blinking.xlsx` | Chewing 4-3-2-1 times, then blinking 4-3-2-1 times | 10,000 samples (40 s) |
| `2.Chewing_Blinking.xlsx` | A second recording of the same test | 10,000 samples (40 s) |
| `3.Alpha_test.xlsx` | Eyes closed for 5 seconds, open for 5 seconds, repeated | 20,000 samples (80 s) |

To plot them, use the scripts in [`Save_data/dataset_visualisation`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/PiEEG-16/tree/main/Save_data/dataset_visualisation). To clean SPI glitch spikes from your own recordings, use [`Dataset/Remove_Spikes.py`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/PiEEG-16/blob/main/Dataset/Remove_Spikes.py).

## Machine learning: stress detection

A complete beginner pipeline in [`ML_Application`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/PiEEG-16/tree/main/ML_Application): record, turn raw EEG into alpha power, then train a classifier.

| Step | Script | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Collect** | `1.Save_Data.py` | Record raw 16-channel EEG to Excel. Aim for about 50 recordings of 1 minute or more for each condition (for example, *stress* and *no stress*). |
| 2. **Extract features** | `2.Convert_to_Power_Alpha.py` | Convert each recording to alpha-band power, in 1-second windows. You can do the same for beta, delta and theta. |
| 3. **Prepare** | | Combine the recordings into one file per condition, and add a column with the recording (or subject) number. |
| 4. **Train** | `3.ML.py` | Train and test a classifier on the two conditions. |

Each recording is saved with one column per channel:

![Example of a collected dataset: one column per channel](/images/hardware/2026/10/pieeg16_dataset_format.jpg)

<Callout type="warning">
  This is a learning example. A classifier trained on a few recordings is not a validated measure of stress, emotion or health.
</Callout>
