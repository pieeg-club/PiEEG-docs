# Signals & Datasets

What real IronBCI-32 recordings look like, and open data to practise with.

## Example signals

### 32 channels in real time

All 32 channels streaming in BrainFlow's real-time plot.

![32 channels of EEG from IronBCI-32 in BrainFlow](/images/hardware/2026/10/ironbci32_brainflow.png)

### EEG: alpha test

Eyes closed and open in turn, recorded with dry Ag/AgCl electrodes without gel. The bottom plot is the signal; the top is its wavelet power. The alpha power rises in the "closed" periods.

![Alpha test with IronBCI-32: eyes closed and open, with wavelet power spectrum](/images/hardware/2026/10/ironbci32_alpha.png)

<Callout type="info">
  Learn to recognise artifacts (blinks, jaw clenches, movement) before you analyse anything. Remove or mark them before computing band power or training a model.
</Callout>

## Dataset

In the [`dataset`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/ironbci-32/tree/main/dataset) folder of the IronBCI-32 repository. Recorded with the [PiEEG 32-channel wet cap](https://www.pieeg.com/hardware/32ch-wet-cap).

| File | Content | Size |
|---|---|---|
| `eeg_32ch_20s.xlsx` | Raw EEG, 32 channels (`Ch1`–`Ch32`), in µV | 10,241 samples, about 20 seconds |

Channel numbers follow the [electrode layout](/hardware/devices/ironbci-32/connections#electrode-layout).

<Callout type="info">
  Raw values carry a large DC offset (tens of thousands of µV). That is normal for raw data. Apply a high-pass or band-pass filter (for example 1–40 Hz) before looking at EEG. [`Plot_data.py`](https://github.com/pieeg-club/ironbci-32/tree/main/SDK/Not_real_time) shows how, with an 8–12 Hz alpha filter and a wavelet view.
</Callout>
