# Contact Calibration

EEG is easy to record badly. A loose electrode, dry hair under a pad or a cable picking up mains hum can ruin a session, and you often won't notice until you look at the data afterwards.

**Contact Calibration** is a quick pre-flight check in the dashboard. It reads the live stream from your PiEEG device and shows, channel by channel, whether the signal is clean enough to record. It takes a few seconds, and you start recording knowing your data is good.

## Supported devices

The device is detected automatically and its layout is selected for you (marked **DETECTED**). You can also switch layouts manually:

| Layout | Channels |
|--------|----------|
| Generic 8 (PiEEG-8) | 8 |
| Generic 16 (PiEEG-16) | 16 |
| Generic 32 | 32 |
| PiEEG XR | XR headset layout |
| Octopus 16 | 16 |

## How to use it

<Steps>

### Put on the electrodes

Place the electrodes in the 10-20 positions for your device and connect the board.

### Open Contact Calibration

In the dashboard, open **Contact Calibration**. Streaming starts and the head map lights up within a second or two.

### Fix anything that isn't green

Each electrode on the head map is colored by its signal quality. If a channel shows **Fair**, **Poor**, **Railed** or **No contact**, adjust that electrode (press it down, part the hair, add gel or saline) and watch the color update live.

### Record

When the badge in the top-right says **Good contact** and all channels are ready, click **Back to dashboard** and start recording.

</Steps>

## Reading the screen

### Head map

A top-down view of the head (nose at the top) with each electrode at its 10-20 position. Every electrode shows its channel number, label (Fp1, C3, O1…) and current SNR in dB. The glow color runs from red (low SNR) to green (high SNR).

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 🟢 **Good** | Clean signal, ready to record |
| 🟡 **Fair** | Usable, but contact could be better |
| 🟠 **Poor** | Noisy, fix before recording |
| 🔴 **Railed** | Signal saturated at the amplifier limit, usually a loose or disconnected electrode |
| ⚫ **No contact** | No signal from this electrode |

### Summary

- **Channels ready**: how many channels currently have good contact (for example `8/8`).
- **Mean SNR**: average signal-to-noise ratio across all channels.
- A one-line verdict telling you whether you're ready to record.

### Channel table

| Column | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **CH** | Electrode label |
| **µV PP** | Peak-to-peak amplitude of the signal, in microvolts |
| **LINE** | Mains (power-line) pickup at 50/60 Hz. Close to `0.0` is best |
| **SNR** | In-band EEG signal compared with the dominant noise source, in dB. Higher is better |
| **STATUS** | Good / Fair / Poor / Railed / No contact |

<Callout type="info">
  These are within-session contact estimates calculated from the live stream. The PiEEG front-end does not measure electrode impedance directly, so Contact Calibration judges contact by signal quality instead.
</Callout>

## Tips for clean data

- **High LINE value**: move away from chargers and power cables, run on battery, and check the reference/bias electrode.
- **Railed channel**: the electrode is usually off the skin or the wire is loose. Reseat it.
- **Low SNR on one channel only**: that electrode needs better contact. If every channel is low, check the reference and ground first.
- **Stay still** for a few seconds while checking. Blinks and jaw clenching briefly lower SNR on frontal and temporal channels.
